Retail Sales Performance

A three-page sales report for a multi-region retailer. It replaces a weekly spreadsheet pack with one place to see revenue, orders and margin, and to drill from region to category to product.

Role
Solo analyst: SQL, modelling, design
Type
Portfolio project
Tools
SQL · Power BI · DAX
Dataset
18.6K orders
01 —The question

What did the business need to know?

We’re growing, but are we growing the right things?

Leadership could see revenue going up, but not where the growth came from or whether it was profitable. The weekly spreadsheet pack took hours to build and couldn’t answer either question.

02 —The data

What I had to work with.

Order, product, customer and region tables, joined in SQL views so revenue and margin reconcile with finance.

03 —The approach

From raw tables to one trusted model.

  1. Step 01

    Build reconciled SQL views

    Joined orders, products, customers and regions; handled returns so revenue and margin tie out with finance.

    SQL: net sales view+ 
    -- Net revenue and margin per order line, returns netted out
create or replace view v_sales as
select
    o.order_id,
    o.order_date,
    r.region_name,
    p.category,
    o.quantity * o.unit_price                    as gross_revenue,
    coalesce(rt.refund_amount, 0)                as refunds,
    o.quantity * o.unit_price
        - coalesce(rt.refund_amount, 0)          as net_revenue,
    o.quantity * (o.unit_price - p.unit_cost)    as gross_margin
from orders o
join products p      on p.product_id = o.product_id
join regions  r      on r.region_id  = o.region_id
left join returns rt on rt.order_line_id = o.order_line_id;
  2. Step 02

    Measure contribution, not just totals

    DAX for revenue, margin, AOV and YoY; category and region contribution to separate volume from value.

  3. Step 03

    One filter set, three audiences

    An overview for leadership, a detail table for category managers and a breakdown for regional teams.

04 —The insights
Finding 01

Electronics sells the most, and earns the least on each sale.

The top revenue category runs well below the business’s 31% average margin. Growth there is costing profit.

Finding 02

Growth came from more orders, not bigger baskets.

Average order value stayed flat around $226 while order volume climbed, so basket-building is an untapped lever.

05 —The impact

What changed, and what I recommended.

31%gross margin, now visible by line
+12%YoY growth, traced to order volume
3 → 1reports replaced by one model
  1. Review pricing and supplier terms on high-volume, low-margin Electronics lines.
  2. Test basket-building promotions to lift AOV rather than discounting for volume.
  3. Set region-level targets from the contribution analysis, not last year plus a flat percentage.
