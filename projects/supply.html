Supply Chain & Inventory

An operations dashboard covering shipments, suppliers and inventory across four warehouses. It replaces three Excel trackers with a single view the team checks every morning.

Role
Solo analyst: SQL, Excel, design
Type
Portfolio project
Tools
Excel · SQL · Power BI
Dataset
2,450 SKUs · 8 suppliers
01 —The question

What did the business need to know?

Why do we keep running out, and who should we call about it?

Stockouts kept surprising the operations team, and nobody could say whether demand or suppliers were to blame. The goal: spot the late shipment before the customer does.

02 —The data

03 —The approach

From raw tables to one trusted model.

  1. Step 01

    Consolidate the trackers

    Moved three Excel trackers into SQL tables with consistent supplier and SKU keys.

  2. Step 02

    Measure suppliers, then stock

    On-time rate and lead time per supplier; SKUs below their reorder point flagged against average daily demand.

    SQL: reorder-point flag+ 
    -- Reorder point from each supplier's actual lead time
with demand as (
    select sku, avg(units_shipped) as avg_daily_demand
    from shipments_daily
    group by sku
),
lead as (
    select supplier_id, avg(received_date - order_date) as avg_lead_days
    from purchase_orders
    group by supplier_id
)
select s.sku, s.warehouse, s.on_hand,
       round(d.avg_daily_demand * l.avg_lead_days * 1.2) as reorder_point,
       s.on_hand < d.avg_daily_demand * l.avg_lead_days * 1.2 as below_reorder
from stock s
join demand d on d.sku = s.sku
join lead   l on l.supplier_id = s.supplier_id;
  3. Step 03

    Exceptions first

    Late shipments and low-stock SKUs are highlighted, with drill-through to supplier and warehouse detail.

04 —The insights
Finding 01

Two suppliers account for most late deliveries.

The other six deliver on time consistently. The problem is concentrated, so the fix can be too.

Finding 02

Stockouts follow long lead times, not demand spikes.

They cluster in the weeks after slow supplier deliveries. Meanwhile one warehouse holds excess slow-movers that another is short of.

05 —The impact

What changed, and what I recommended.

92.4%on-time delivery, now tracked daily
37stockouts traced to root cause
3 → 1Excel trackers replaced
  1. Renegotiate SLAs, or add backup suppliers, for the two least reliable suppliers.
  2. Set reorder points from actual lead time per supplier instead of a single default.
  3. Rebalance stock between warehouses weekly using the excess/shortage view.
